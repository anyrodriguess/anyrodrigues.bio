From stem cells to a living neural interface

A proposed research direction for Axo Neurotech, grounded in published muscle-cell programming, flexible microelectrode arrays and peripheral nerve electrophysiology.

Applicant Any Rodrigues   |   Focus Cell engineering, electrophysiology and reproducibility   |   Status Independent proposal, not completed Axo research

Why this interests me

My experience maintaining neural cultures at DZNE and preparing adult zebrafish retina for high-density MEA recordings at CRTD made me interested in how biological preparation determines whether an electrical measurement is meaningful. The work by Rochford and colleagues [2] offers a different kind of interface: living muscle cells are incorporated into flexible electronics to create a biological target for peripheral nerve inputs. I would like to contribute to the preparation, recording quality and experimental reproducibility of systems like this, while learning the methods specific to Axo.

What the paper showed, and what it did not

Demonstrated in Rochford et al. (2023)

  • Human iPSC-derived myocytes were integrated with a flexible parylene-based, 32-electrode MEA and fibrin hydrogel.
  • Human-derived cells and neuromuscular junction markers were identified after four weeks in a rat peripheral nerve model.
  • Stimulation-evoked compound action potentials were recorded at 28 days from the biohybrid interface, with comparisons to cell-free controls.
  • Chronic recordings over four weeks showed improving signal-to-noise ratios in the reported animals.

Not yet established by that study

  • Performance and tissue response over clinically relevant months or years.
  • Manufacturing yield and batch-to-batch consistency at scale.
  • Generalisation across cell lines, nerve types, patients or surgical conditions.
  • Clinical safety and therapeutic effectiveness in humans.

Important distinction: the authors propose mechanisms for improved recording and integration, but these do not establish that every observed electrical change is caused by a single biological mechanism. The 2023 publication is not proof of Axo's current product performance. [2]

Experimental design, not simulation: A real pilot would define the biological replicate (independent preparation or differentiation batch), appropriate reference controls, blinded or consistent scoring where feasible, acquisition and exclusion criteria, and a model-appropriate functional readout before collecting data. Electrode count does not equal biological sample size. In vitro measurements alone cannot prove long-term in vivo integration.

My proposed 3, 6 and 12-month contribution

By month 3

Learn approved cell and interface workflows, document preparation and recording quality, establish a reproducible baseline.

Output: QC checklist, baseline dataset and variability report.

By month 6

Compare one team-approved preparation variable with a reference, pairing biological observations and electrophysiology.

Output: Controlled pilot, uncertainty analysis and follow-up recommendation.

By month 12

Evaluate stability and repeatability, document failure modes and refine the protocol with colleagues.

Output: Reproducible workflow, stability evidence and technical handover.

Questions I would ask the team

  1. Which part of the published biohybrid approach has changed most as you have moved towards translation?
  2. Is the immediate experimental bottleneck biological preparation, functional integration, recording fidelity or reproducibility?
  3. Which readout best demonstrates that a biological interface improves the device rather than simply changing electrode properties?
  4. How are cell batches and interface preparations currently qualified before further testing?
  5. What evidence would be most useful to make a go or no-go decision on a candidate interface condition?
