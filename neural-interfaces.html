The proposed 3-, 6- and 12-month work plan

12-month execution timeline

Overall success criterion: My work would be valuable if it enables Axo to make a better-informed design or experimental decision. More cells, more spikes or one attractive trace alone would not demonstrate a better interface; biological quality, signal integrity, model-appropriate function and repeatability must be interpreted together.
MONTHS 1–3

Establish a trustworthy baseline

What and how: Train on approved cell and device SOPs; document preparation conditions; pair microscopy observations with raw recording inspection; log noise, artifacts and exclusions; repeat baseline measurements across independent preparations where feasible.

Deliverables: Versioned culture/recording checklist, metadata and QC template, initial baseline dataset and short variability report.

Success looks like: The team can reproduce the workflow and identify when a recording is interpretable, why it is excluded and which technical or biological factors limit data quality.

Finding enabled: Which sources of variation come from the living preparation, the interface or the recording system?

MONTHS 4–6

Test one meaningful variable

What and how: Select a feasible, team-approved variable (for example a culture-handling or interface condition). Define reference and test groups, biological replicate units, relevant controls and endpoints before collecting paired imaging and electrical data.

Deliverables: Controlled pilot dataset, plots showing variation and effect sizes, documented limitations and a recommendation to retain, revise or stop the tested condition.

Success looks like: A decision is supported by interpretable comparisons, including negative or inconclusive results, without mistaking technical artifacts for biology.

Finding enabled: Does the tested condition correspond to a consistent change in biological health, electrical signal quality or model-appropriate functional response?

MONTHS 7–12

Assess stability and repeatability

What and how: Follow selected preparations at a feasible recording cadence; examine electrical drift alongside biological observations; repeat the comparison across independent preparations or operators when possible; document failure modes.

Deliverables: Longitudinal or repeatability dataset, stability figures, revised SOP/QC documentation, reproducible analysis files and final technical handover.

Success looks like: The team knows what is stable, what fails and what should be changed in the next design iteration. A positive outcome is not required.

Finding enabled: Which conditions support sustained, repeatable biological–electrical measurements, and where are the limits?

Research objective. I would investigate how culture and interface conditions relate to the quality, functionality and stability of electrical measurements from living cell–electrode systems. My role would be to generate reproducible biological and electrophysiological evidence that helps the team decide which interface conditions to test, improve or discontinue. The exact cell model, device and approved endpoints would be agreed with Axo before experiments begin.

Experimental approach and interpretation

The bars show proposed activity durations and overlaps, not fixed commitments. Milestone reviews at months 3, 6, 9 and 12 allow the team to adjust the plan to experimental evidence.

Decision gates and practical constraints

Biology and interface observations

Track preparation identity, handling, culture age and microscopy observations. Use viability or attachment measures only when validated for the selected cell model and interface.

Electrical quality and function

Inspect raw traces, noise, artifacts and usable channels. If the preparation supports it, assess spontaneous or stimulus-evoked activity with appropriate controls. Spiking and bursting are not universal endpoints for every biohybrid interface.

Controlled comparisons

Define biological replicates separately from electrodes and repeated measurements. Compare matched conditions, examine effect sizes and uncertainty, and document missing or excluded data.

Longitudinal interpretation

Pair repeated electrical measurements with biological observations; distinguish time-dependent drift, preparation loss and batch effects. Do not infer causation from a simple correlation.

What Axo would gain from my work

Scope and responsibility: I can contribute directly to culture maintenance after training, preparation and microscopy logs, MEA acquisition, data QC and exploratory analysis. Device-specific fabrication, stimulation limits, specialised biointerface assays and experimental approvals would remain with the appropriate Axo specialists. This proposal does not assume access to proprietary hardware protocols, implants or animal experiments.

Main risks: culture-to-culture variability, recording artifacts, limited independent replicates and cell models for which standard neuronal MEA metrics are inappropriate. I would address these through documented SOPs, raw-signal checks, model-appropriate controls and early reviews with biology and engineering colleagues.
