Biological Computation

When living neurons become part of a computer

The difference between biological experiments, hybrid systems and general-purpose computing.

About this page: An independent overview of a scientific topic I am interested in, not a claim that I have performed all the research discussed here.

01 / The core idea

Living neuronal networks respond to electrical input and generate measurable activity. By translating stimulation into input and recorded activity into output, researchers can connect a culture to a digital environment.

02 / DishBrain was two-dimensional

In 2022, Kagan and colleagues used 2D neuronal cultures on an electrode array to interact with a simplified Pong environment. They reported adaptation during closed-loop feedback. DishBrain did not use a 3D brain organoid. [1]

03 / Cortical Labs

Cortical Labs develops biological computing systems including CL1. Its Cortical Cloud initiative focuses on remote access to experimental systems. Company performance and efficiency claims should be evaluated against published benchmarks.

04 / FinalSpark

FinalSpark describes a remote Neuroplatform for experiments involving living human neural tissue, including organoids. Its 2024 paper outlines the platform and its intended research use. [2]

05 / What is not yet demonstrated

Experimental neural interfaces are not evidence that biological systems can replace general-purpose silicon computers. Scalability, reliability, standardization, lifespan, total energy use and meaningful task benchmarks are open challenges.

06 / Why this interests me

I am particularly interested in the interface between neural electrophysiology and closed-loop systems: how to design inputs, measure responses and test whether changes reflect genuine adaptation. For the wider scientific story, see Organoid Intelligence.
