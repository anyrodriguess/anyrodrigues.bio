Master's Thesis

Establishing CMOS-MEA Recordings in Adult Zebrafish Retina

During my master's thesis at the Center for Regenerative Therapies Dresden, I worked on establishing extracellular electrophysiological recordings from isolated adult zebrafish retina using a high-density CMOS microelectrode array with 4,096 electrodes.

The project involved optimizing retinal dissection, tissue placement and recording conditions to obtain reliable neuronal activity. I investigated spontaneous and light-evoked responses, characterized ON, OFF and ON-OFF activity, and examined the effects of pharmacological modulation and light-induced retinal injury.

I also developed and applied data analysis workflows for spike activity, firing rates, stimulus responses and spatial patterns of neuronal activity.

Research Internship

Olfactory Neural Cultures and Electrophysiological Responses to Odorant Stimulation

At the German Center for Neurodegenerative Diseases (DZNE), I worked with olfactory epithelium and olfactory bulb cell cultures, including co-culture configurations designed to explore interactions between these neural populations.

My work included preparing and maintaining cultures, monitoring them through microscopy, preparing microelectrode arrays, and recording neuronal activity following exposure to odorant compounds such as eugenol and isoamyl acetate.

The broader objective was to investigate whether odorant stimulation could produce distinguishable electrophysiological response patterns, with potential relevance to the development of olfactory biosensing approaches. Although the responses were variable, the project provided experience in neural culture models, assay development and electrophysiological data interpretation.

Research Interests

I am particularly interested in developing experimental models that can help us understand disease mechanisms and evaluate potential therapies.

Building on my experience in electrophysiology and neural cultures, I would like to expand into organoids, organ-on-chip systems and other advanced in vitro models, especially where functional measurements can complement cellular and molecular characterization.
