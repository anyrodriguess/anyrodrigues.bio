NeuroDose.

Can a living neuronal network use feedback to select useful interventions in a simulated model of Dravet syndrome?

Dravet syndrome / SCN1AFenfluramine benchmarkZorevunersen contextClosed-loop biological computing
Proposed research — no experimental results. This is a literature-grounded, hypothetical preclinical protocol and portfolio research dossier. No cultures, dosing experiments, human trials or biological controller tests have been performed for NeuroDose. Simulated outcomes must be labelled as such. The project does not establish efficacy, safety, or superiority over existing preclinical methods.

Central distinction: One biological system models the disease and drug response. A separate neuronal reservoir acts as a candidate controller of a virtual intervention. Separating these roles prevents drug-induced changes in the assay from being mistaken for controller learning.

01 / Clinical context

Why Dravet syndrome?

Dravet syndrome is a developmental and epileptic encephalopathy commonly associated with loss-of-function variants in SCN1A, which encodes the NaV1.1 sodium channel. Impaired inhibitory interneuron development and function contribute to circuit dysfunction. Network-wide firing rate alone is not an adequate surrogate for clinical seizures: cellular models can exhibit reduced inhibitory activity while the intact circuit is seizure-prone.

NEAR-TERM ASSAY ANCHOR

Fenfluramine

Approved antiseizure medicine. A 2026 study of patient-derived inhibitory forebrain organoids reported partial restoration of neuronal activity after acute fenfluramine application. The reported readouts included patch clamp and calcium imaging; do not present them as HD-MEA data.

DISEASE-MODIFYING CONTEXT

Zorevunersen

Investigational antisense oligonucleotide intended to increase NaV1.1 expression. The Phase 3 EMPEROR trial was ongoing in October 2026, with a readout anticipated in Q3 2027. Its delayed molecular effects make it unsuitable as a simplistic instant-response dosing controller.

Working decision: Use fenfluramine-related functional rescue as the initial literature anchor for a drug-response model; treat zorevunersen as a separate, slower-timescale translational scenario rather than pretending it has acute dose-response dynamics.

02 / Scientific precedent

What has been done already?

Smirnova / DROIDp

Organoid intelligence and neurotoxicity: functional plasticity and drug-sensitive learning-related endpoints. A crucial neighboring direction, but not evidence that organoids have autonomously optimized clinical dosing.

Brainoware

Brain organoids as reservoirs receiving electrical input, with an external computational readout for tasks such as speech recognition and nonlinear prediction.

DishBrain

Closed-loop behavior in dissociated neuronal cultures interacting with Pong. It is not an experiment with 3D brain organoids.

Dravet inhibitory organoids (2026)

Patient-derived subpallial organoids with disrupted interneuron maturation and partial acute fenfluramine rescue: a relevant disease-model precedent, not an autonomous dosing study.

03 / Questions

Research aim & falsifiable hypotheses

Primary aim: Test whether a simulated or eventually biological neuronal reservoir can contribute to action selection in a virtual Dravet-related pharmacological environment, beyond conventional computational controllers.

H1 — Functional disease phenotype

Prespecified electrophysiological features distinguish patient-derived SCN1A models from appropriately matched controls.

H2 — Drug response

Multiparametric responses can distinguish partial functional normalization from indiscriminate neuronal silencing.

H3 — Biological computation

Neuronal reservoir activity contributes predictive information beyond raw state features and random or shuffled reservoirs.

H4 — Feedback-dependent adaptation

Contingent feedback improves performance on held-out environments beyond yoked feedback, frozen readouts and standard control algorithms.

04 / Proposed wet-lab architecture

Two separate biological roles

A. Disease assay

Human iPSC-derived SCN1A-variant inhibitory forebrain organoids, matched controls and ideally isogenic-corrected lines. Characterize cell-type composition, maturation, viability and reproducible evoked activity. For HD-MEA recording, test validated organoid interfaces or slices; intact organoids on planar arrays do not provide uniform access to all neurons.

B. Candidate living controller

A separate, well-characterized neuronal culture or organoid-derived preparation on a stimulation/recording MEA. The controller receives encoded virtual disease-state observations, produces an electrophysiological response and feeds an external, prespecified action decoder.

Biological replication: Donors, independent differentiations and organoids matter. Electrodes, detected units and thousands of time windows are not independent biological replicates. A sample size is not fixed until pilot variability and a power calculation are available.
05 / Drug-response modelling

What exactly would the controller control?

For the first simulation, the action is a bounded adjustment to virtual exposure, not a recommendation for a real patient or an instruction to physically dose an organoid. The virtual state contains a functional disease score, exposure history, delayed response and uncertainty.

Critical scientific question: Does adaptation occur in the living network, in the software decoder, or only in the simulated environment? Each must be evaluated separately. The biological meaning of “reward” stimulation is not established by simply assigning it a positive label.

Primary simulated endpoint

Pre-register a composite score balancing time in a prespecified functional target range, pathological network features, excessive suppression, cumulative virtual exposure and controller instability. Also report each component separately to prevent a misleading single score.

Statistics

Use replicate-level comparisons, hierarchical or mixed-effects models where appropriate, held-out parameter sets, bootstrap confidence intervals and sensitivity analyses. Never treat spikes or windows from one organoid as independent donor samples.

09 / What would count as success?

Evidence ladder

  1. Reproducible disease phenotype: independent SCN1A models differ from matched controls in predefined measurements.
  2. Pharmacological sensitivity: drug-associated functional shifts are distinguishable from sedation/silencing and experimental drift.
  3. Useful reservoir representation: real neural responses add predictive value beyond raw state features.
  4. Contingency-specific adaptation: feedback effects persist on held-out tasks and exceed yoked-feedback controls.
  5. Preclinical advantage: a prospective, independently replicated comparison shows added value versus strong nonbiological baselines.
Even satisfying these criteria would not prove clinical benefit or establish safe autonomous drug dosing. The first project phase is a computational proof-of-concept, not a clinical trial.
10 / Project roadmap

Build it like a real research project

  1. Literature matrix. Extract Dravet pathophysiology, cell-model evidence, fenfluramine response and zorevunersen mechanism; log which facts are established and which are assumptions. NEXT
  2. Define phenotype. Specify the electrophysiological readouts and how inhibitory organoid measurements relate to network-level disease.
  3. Source quantitative data. Search supplementary figures/tables and open datasets; document gaps rather than inventing empirical parameters.
  4. Build the virtual assay. Simulate disease-state dynamics and delayed intervention effects with uncertainty.
  5. Benchmark standard algorithms. Fixed policy, PID, Bayesian selection or RL where appropriate.
  6. Build the neural reservoir simulator. Specify input encoding, spike generation, decoding and biological plausibility checks.
  7. Test the closed loop. Contingent vs yoked feedback; unseen conditions; reproducibility across random seeds.
  8. Generate figures and manuscript. Report null results, limitations and synthetic provenance explicitly.
  9. Future wet-lab feasibility plan. Specify actual HD-MEA interfaces, stimulation artefact controls, biological replication and ethics/oversight.
