Could Living Neural Networks Transform Preclinical Drug Testing?

NeuroDose — A proposed closed-loop organoid intelligence framework.

Can functional electrophysiology from human disease-model neural networks guide which drug concentration should be tested next, while preserving healthy network activity?

Project status: Conceptual experimental design and literature review. No patient-derived organoids, drug exposures or closed-loop biological experiments have been performed as part of NeuroDose. The proposed results below are hypotheses, not findings.

01 / Research question

Drug screening often relies on fixed concentration series and endpoint measurements. Neural cultures on microelectrode arrays offer an additional dimension: functional responses over time. NeuroDose proposes an adaptive testing strategy that uses these responses to decide which experimental concentration to investigate next.

Hypothesis: An algorithm informed by neuronal activity and safety criteria could identify informative concentrations with fewer experimental conditions than a conventional fixed concentration series. This must be tested against a matched baseline; it is not an established advantage.

Two separate goals: (A) use biological recordings to guide drug-testing decisions; (B) investigate whether drug exposure alters the neural network's capacity for experience-dependent adaptation. Goal A does not, by itself, demonstrate that the organoid learns.

02 / Why Dravet syndrome?

Dravet syndrome is a developmental and epileptic encephalopathy frequently associated with pathogenic variants in SCN1A , which encodes the Na V 1.1 sodium channel. Disease mechanisms can involve impaired inhibitory interneuron excitability and altered network dynamics, although phenotypes vary with genotype, cell type and model maturity.

Proposed biological comparison: patient-derived iPSC neural cultures or organoids with a well-characterised SCN1A variant, alongside a genetically matched isogenic control. The first practical choice would be a validated preparation with sufficient inhibitory neuron representation and reproducible MEA activity—not simply the most anatomically complex organoid.

Disease-associated model

Characterise spontaneous firing, burst dynamics, synchrony and stimulus-evoked responses.

Isogenic control

Establish reference ranges and distinguish disease-associated changes from maturation and batch effects.

03 / How the proposed closed loop works

1. Biological network
Human disease-model culture / organoid on a suitable MEA interface
↓
2. Record
Spikes, bursts, synchrony, stimulus responses and recording quality
↓
3. Interpret
Compare with control distributions, previous exposures and safety criteria
↓
4. Select next test
Choose among predefined candidate concentrations or request a replicate
↓
5. Experiment and update
Apply to an appropriately controlled culture; record time-dependent response; update the model
↺ REPEAT WITH NEW DATA

This is a slow pharmacological feedback loop : the software chooses the next experiment using prior measurements. It is distinct from a fast electrophysiological loop where recorded spikes trigger electrical stimulation in real time.

A biohybrid architecture such as BiœmuS motivates the feasibility of real-time neural interfacing, but its published hybridisation demonstration does not validate adaptive drug concentration selection.

04 / From real compounds to interpretable responses

The biological proposal uses actual therapeutic candidates, not fictional compounds. Candidate selection and concentration ranges must be justified by published in-vitro exposure studies and assay-specific pharmacology before experiments begin.

Candidate Why investigate it? Evidence still needed
Fenfluramine Clinically used for seizures associated with Dravet syndrome; a candidate for studying functional network responses. Relevant human neural model data, exposure concentrations, duration, assay compatibility and toxicity.
Cannabidiol Clinically used for Dravet-associated seizures; useful to examine efficacy versus nonspecific suppression. Free concentration, protein binding, vehicle effects, solubility and model-specific dose-response evidence.
Stiripentol Another established Dravet therapy; potential mechanistic comparator. Culture-relevant exposure evidence and validated functional endpoints.
Why no µM values yet? A clinical dose in mg cannot be directly translated into the concentration bathing an organoid. We will add numerical concentration ranges only after checking primary experimental studies, exposure time, medium binding, solubility and cell viability. This is an intentional evidence gap, not an experimental result.

Proposed functional readouts

Mean firing rate; burst rate and duration; network synchrony; evoked response reliability; activity recovery; and independent viability measurements. A fall in bursting is not necessarily beneficial if the entire network becomes silent.

Illustrative decision rules

Observed response Next experimental decision
Pathological activity decreases while baseline function and viability remain acceptable Replicate and compare with controls; consider nearby candidate concentrations.
No meaningful response, acceptable viability Consider another prespecified concentration or compound.
Global silencing, unstable recording or reduced viability Flag as possible toxicity / nonspecific suppression; do not treat as rescue.
Ambiguous or highly variable result Repeat with independent cultures before changing the experimental range.

Concentrations would preferably be evaluated in independent cultures to limit carryover and cumulative exposure effects. The algorithm selects experiments; it does not autonomously prescribe medication to people.

05 / Where does biological learning enter?

Adaptive dose selection primarily demonstrates learning by the external algorithm . To study learning in the living network, NeuroDose proposes a separate exploratory task: deliver structured electrical input, decode a defined response, and return contingent feedback. Compare task performance before and after exposure to determine whether a compound changes adaptation or retention.

Open-loop comparison

Replay a fixed stimulation sequence regardless of neuronal activity. Measure responsiveness and stability.

Closed-loop condition

Use measured activity to update stimulation or feedback according to a prespecified task. Test whether performance improves beyond controls.

Critical distinction: persistent activity changes, habituation, plasticity and improved game performance are not interchangeable. A learning claim would require appropriate controls, a stable decoder, reproducible behavioural metrics and tests of retention or generalisation.

06 / What would make the findings credible?

  • Biological controls: isogenic controls, vehicle-only cultures, independent differentiation batches and organoid/culture replicates.
  • Pharmacological controls: prespecified concentration range, matched exposure times, viability, washout/recovery when appropriate and vehicle effects.
  • Electrical controls: stimulation artefact rejection, stable electrode quality, baseline drift, recording stability and sham stimulation.
  • Algorithm controls: fixed-dose grid, random or non-adaptive selection, held-out validation and transparent decision rules.
  • Learning controls: open-loop or replayed-feedback conditions, fixed decoder, retention tests and separation of biological from software adaptation.

Primary endpoints and success criteria would be registered before data collection. A positive result should not be defined after inspecting the recordings.

08 / Scientific foundation

  1. Beaubois et al. (2024), Nature Communications — BiœmuS . Real-time biomimetic spiking networks and hybrid interactions with living neural systems; technical inspiration, not proof of adaptive drug testing.
  2. Kagan et al. (2022), Neuron — DishBrain . Closed-loop interaction and adaptive behaviour in 2D neuronal cultures; a conceptual reference for the separate learning aim.
  3. Guo et al. (2023), Nature Electronics — Brainoware . Brain-organoid reservoir computing; illustrates a distinct biohybrid architecture.
  4. Jordan et al. (2024), Frontiers in Artificial Intelligence — Neuroplatform . Remote neural-organoid interfaces and practical experimental constraints.

Evidence still to collect: Dravet-specific human iPSC/organoid MEA phenotypes, primary in-vitro fenfluramine/cannabidiol concentration-response studies, and published functional safety thresholds. These are required before any numerical dose table can be defended.
