Functional Characterization of Olfactory Neuronal Cultures Using MEAs

Investigating spontaneous and compound-associated electrophysiological activity in olfactory bulb (OB), olfactory epithelium (OE), and OB–OE co-culture preparations.

Research question

Can olfactory neuronal cultures and co-cultures develop measurable spontaneous network activity, and how do their electrophysiological patterns change during experimental compound exposure and washout?

Experimental approach

Culture modelsOB-only, OE-only and OB+OE preparations; pilot comparisons across culture conditions and days in vitro.
RecordingsMEA-based assessment of spontaneous activity and activity during exposure/washout sequences.
AnalysisFiring rate, burst-related metrics, raster visualization and qualitative assessment of network synchrony.

Interpretation and limitations

The pilot experiments demonstrate the practical importance of monitoring network development, recording stability and compound-associated changes in electrophysiological activity. The original notes also document mechanical perturbation during pipetting, possible DMSO effects, differences between fresh and frozen preparations, and variable recovery after exposure. These are important confounders when evaluating whether a change in network activity is truly attributable to a specific treatment.

Relevance to functional phenotyping: The approach illustrates how neuronal preparations can be assessed through firing, bursting and recovery endpoints. Turning this into a robust screening assay would require matched vehicle and handling controls, replicate-level analysis, standardized timing and predefined quantitative response criteria.
