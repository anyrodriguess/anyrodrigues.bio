Olfactory Neural Cultures and Electrophysiological Responses to Odorant Stimulation

RESEARCH INTERNSHIP / 2023–2024

German Center for Neurodegenerative Diseases (DZNE), Dresden, Germany.

During my research internship at DZNE, I investigated olfactory neural cultures using microelectrode arrays to record spontaneous activity and responses to chemical stimulation.

The project explored whether olfactory epithelium and olfactory bulb cell cultures, including co-culture preparations, could produce distinguishable electrophysiological response patterns when exposed to different odorant compounds.

NEURAL CULTURE MODELS

OLFACTORY EPITHELIUM (OE)

Olfactory epithelium-derived cell cultures were used to investigate activity associated with the olfactory system.

OLFACTORY BULB (OB)

Olfactory bulb-derived neural cultures were maintained and characterized through microscopy and extracellular recordings.

CO-CULTURES

OE and OB cells were combined in different proportions to explore how culture composition influenced network activity and experimental responses.

My practical work included culture preparation, maintenance, media changes, microscopy and monitoring of culture development.

MEA EXPERIMENTS

ELECTROPHYSIOLOGY

Neural activity was monitored using microelectrode arrays, primarily with Axion BioSystems equipment.

ODORANT STIMULATION

Cultures were exposed to odorant compounds, including eugenol and isoamyl acetate, to examine changes in their electrophysiological activity.

EXPERIMENTAL COMPARISONS

Activity patterns were evaluated across culture configurations and stimulation conditions.

DATA COLLECTION

Recordings were inspected and analyzed to characterize spiking, bursting and changes in activity.

ANALYZING OLFACTORY NEURAL ACTIVITY

Electrophysiological recordings were analyzed to investigate whether exposure to different compounds produced characteristic changes in network activity.

MEASUREMENTS INCLUDED:

  • Mean firing rate (MFR)
  • Spike counts
  • Inter-spike intervals (ISI)
  • Mean burst rate and burst characteristics
  • Inter-burst intervals (IBI)
  • Comparisons between baseline and stimulation

These measurements helped characterize neural activity across preparations and assess experimental variability.

RESEARCH OBJECTIVE AND FINDINGS

01 / The research question

Could the electrical activity of olfactory neural cultures provide a measurable response to odorants that might eventually be useful for biological sensing?

02 / Experimental exploration

Different cell culture preparations and odorant stimulation conditions were investigated to identify changes in electrophysiological activity.

03 / Variability

Although neuronal activity and experimental responses could be recorded, variability across preparations prevented the establishment of a consistent, reproducible odorant-specific electrophysiological fingerprint.

04 / Broader relevance

The work contributed to exploring biological approaches to olfactory sensing, with a longer-term interest in applications relevant to neurodegenerative disease research.

This project used neural culture and co-culture preparations on microelectrode arrays. It was not a completed organ-on-chip device or a validated diagnostic platform.
