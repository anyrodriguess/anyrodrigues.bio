RESEARCH / DZNE / 2023–2024

Olfactory Co-cultures & Odorant-Evoked Electrophysiology

Could different odorants produce recognizable patterns of neural electrical activity? At DZNE Dresden, I explored this question through olfactory bulb and olfactory epithelium cultures, microelectrode array recordings and chemical stimulation.

Neural cell cultureAxion MEA3Brain CMOS-MEAEugenolIsoamyl acetate

01 / Why this project?

Changes in smell can appear early in some neurodegenerative diseases. The broader motivation was to explore whether odorant-evoked electrical activity could eventually support more reproducible in vitro olfactory models and, in the longer term, research into early disease-related changes.

My experimental focus was more specific: comparing neuronal activity across different olfactory culture configurations and stimulation conditions to investigate whether consistent odorant-related patterns could be identified.

This was exploratory work, not a validated diagnostic assay or a completed organ-on-chip device.

02 / Experimental models

We investigated olfactory bulb (OB) and olfactory epithelium (OE) cultures, including monocultures and co-cultures. Experimental plans covered rat-derived preparations and combinations of rat OB with human OE, as well as different cell compositions and plating arrangements.

Culture configurations

  • OB-only and OE-only preparations
  • OB + OE co-cultures
  • 30%, 50% and 70% OE conditions (with corresponding OB fractions)
  • Premixed cells and layered co-culture arrangements in experimental plans

Culture maintenance

  • Prepared and maintained cultures over several weeks
  • Performed medium changes
  • Routinely examined cultures under the microscope
  • Prepared and coated MEA surfaces for recordings

03 / From culture to recording

Using primarily Axion BioSystems MEAs, with additional exposure to 3Brain CMOS-MEA platforms, we recorded spontaneous and odorant-evoked activity. The October 2023 experimental plan included repeated 10-minute recordings with washout periods and stimulation around days 19–25 in vitro, depending on the preparation.

05 / Observations & challenges

Preliminary analysis of an October 2023 human-plate experiment noted decreases in several firing and bursting measures across different co-culture compositions, but also exceptions between recordings. This variability was an important limitation of the work.

Across the experiments, responses were too variable to establish a reliable, reproducible odorant-specific electrophysiological fingerprint. The work reinforced how important culture composition, experimental consistency and biological variability are when trying to interpret neural network responses.

Outcome: exploratory electrophysiological responses were investigated; a universal odorant fingerprint was not established.
