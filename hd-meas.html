High-Density Microelectrode Arrays

More electrodes, richer views of neural activity

01 / What changes with electrode density?

High-density MEAs increase the number of spatial sampling sites, potentially enabling more detailed observation of activity across a neural preparation. Electrode count alone does not guarantee better biological information.

02 / Recording preparations

Neural cultures, tissue slices, retinal preparations and some organoid interfaces can be investigated using MEAs. Tissue contact, viability, perfusion and stable experimental conditions strongly influence the quality of recordings.

03 / Analysis

Spike detection, sorting or unit identification, raster plots, firing rates and peri-stimulus time histograms are examples of analyses that can help characterize activity. The available analyses depend on hardware, software and signal quality.

04 / Three-dimensional challenges

Conventional planar arrays primarily access cells close to the electrode surface. Organoids create additional problems for contact and recording from deeper layers, motivating alternative electrode geometries and interfaces.

05 / My hands-on experience

During my Master’s thesis, I established and optimized an ex vivo adult zebrafish retina HD-MEA workflow, including tissue preparation, attachment, recording conditions, visual stimulation and analysis. See Research.
