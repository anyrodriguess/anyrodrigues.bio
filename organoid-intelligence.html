ORGANOID INTELLIGENCE

living neural tissue as a computational system

📁 what_is_organoid_intelligence.txt

Organoid Intelligence (OI) is an emerging field exploring whether three-dimensional cultures of human neural cells can be interfaced with technology and used to study — and potentially perform — forms of biological information processing and computation.

Brain organoids are laboratory-grown 3D neural tissues derived from stem cells. They do not reproduce a complete human brain, but they can develop organised neural populations and spontaneous electrical activity.

OI brings together stem-cell biology, neuroscience, electrophysiology, bioengineering, machine learning and brain-machine interfaces.

⚙ basic_loop.sys

The idea I find particularly fascinating is the possibility of creating a closed loop between a living neural system and a computer.

COMPUTER provides stimulation / input
→
ORGANOID responds through neural activity
→
MEA records electrical activity
↺

By repeatedly stimulating the neural tissue, recording its response and modifying subsequent input, researchers can investigate adaptation and plasticity in a controlled environment.

♥ why_i_am_here.txt

What interests me most about organoid intelligence is the boundary between observing neural activity and interacting with it.

My previous work involved recording electrical activity from living neural tissue using high-density microelectrode arrays. OI feels like a natural extension of that experience: moving from recording neural responses toward creating closed-loop systems in which biological networks can receive information, respond and adapt.

I am especially interested in how electrophysiological activity can be translated into meaningful computational output — and how stimulation can be designed to shape the behaviour of living neural networks.
