MASTER’S THESIS / 2024

Establishing CMOS-MEA recordings in adult zebrafish retina

Center for Regenerative Therapies Dresden (CRTD), TU Dresden

For my master’s thesis, I worked to establish extracellular recordings from isolated adult zebrafish retina using a high-density CMOS microelectrode array. I investigated spontaneous and visually evoked activity, pharmacological modulation, and changes following retinal injury.

01 / Recording preparation

Ex vivo preparation

Adult zebrafish retina was dissected and maintained under perfusion. I investigated tissue positioning, contact with the electrode array, and recording conditions to improve signal detection.

Electrode platform

Recordings used the 3Brain Accura CMOS-MEA with 4,096 electrodes, enabling spatially distributed measurements across the retinal tissue.

02 / Visual stimulation & experimental conditions

Light responses

Constant light and chirp paradigms were used to examine visually evoked activity, including ON, OFF and ON–OFF response patterns.

Pharmacology and injury

Meclofenamic acid (MFA) was used to investigate pharmacological modulation. Light-induced retinal injury was also explored to assess changes in responsiveness.

03 / Signal processing

Analysis workflow

Using Python and R, I worked with spike activity, firing rates, inter-spike intervals, peristimulus time histograms (PSTHs), and comparisons across experimental conditions.

Response classification

Visually responsive units were characterized through changes in activity relative to baseline, including ON, OFF and ON–OFF patterns.

04 / Main findings & limitations

Functional recordings

Optimizing the preparation enabled detection of spontaneous and visually evoked retinal activity on the CMOS-MEA.

Experimental effects

MFA experiments showed changes in light-associated spiking, while injury experiments revealed loss of clear light responsiveness at the later recording point. These results were exploratory and depended on recording conditions.
