Establishing CMOS-MEA Recordings in Adult Zebrafish Retina

For my master's dissertation at the Center for Regenerative Therapies TU Dresden (CRTD), I optimized ex vivo adult zebrafish retinal recordings and investigated how retinal ganglion cells respond to light, pharmacological intervention and acute light-induced damage.

01 / Why zebrafish retina?

Zebrafish can regenerate retinal tissue after injury, making them a valuable model for studying mechanisms of neural repair. While the cellular and molecular aspects of retinal regeneration are widely investigated, measuring the functional electrical responses of adult retinal neurons provides a complementary perspective.

My project focused on retinal ganglion cell (RGC) activity: could we obtain reliable high-resolution recordings, distinguish light-responsive populations and measure the immediate functional effects of experimental perturbations?

02 / Building a reliable recording preparation

Starting from an initial laboratory protocol, I worked on retinal dissection and tissue mounting to improve contact between the ganglion cell layer and CMOS microelectrodes. The preparation used isolated adult zebrafish retina mounted on 3Brain chips, with physiological perfusion during recording.

The dissertation compared tissue preparation and mounting strategies, including vitreous removal, coating, applied pressure and three-dimensional electrode configurations. Improving tissue–electrode contact was central to obtaining more informative recordings.

Platform: 3Brain BioCAM / CMOS-MEA with 4,096 recording electrodes. The project established an experimental workflow rather than a clinical assay.

03 / From electrical signals to functional responses

Acquisition and processing

  • Extracellular spike detection and spike sorting using BrainWave
  • Spike trains, raster plots and mean firing rate (MFR)
  • Interspike intervals, response timing and activity distributions
  • Comparisons between spontaneous baseline and stimulated phases

Light stimulation

  • Repeated white-light step flashes from 0.1 to 1.0 seconds
  • Assessment of changes in spiking activity during light stimulation
  • Classification of responses at light onset (ON), offset (OFF) or both (ON-OFF)
  • Exploration of heterogeneous response profiles across recorded units

Across five experiments summarized in the dissertation, baseline and light-stimulated firing rates differed significantly in four experiments, while one did not show a significant difference. The response patterns also varied with stimulus duration, illustrating the diversity of retinal activity.

04 / Functional classification of retinal ganglion cells

By comparing spike activity around stimulus onset and offset, I characterized units showing ON, OFF and ON-OFF response patterns. This provided an initial functional view of the recorded retinal population rather than treating all electrically active units as equivalent.

ON responses

Increased activity associated with the appearance of light.

OFF responses

Increased activity associated with the disappearance of light.

ON-OFF responses

Responses associated with both stimulus transitions.

Response diversity

Different units exhibited different timing and sensitivity across light-step conditions.

This was an initial electrophysiological classification; it did not establish molecular identities for the recorded cells.

05 / Pharmacological modulation and light lesion

Meclofenamic acid (MFA)

I investigated changes in retinal activity after application of MFA, a gap-junction blocker. The treatment altered recorded firing activity and light responses, providing a pharmacological perturbation of the established recording system.

Acute light-induced lesion

Following intense white-light exposure, spontaneous activity increased, while light-evoked responses were strongly impaired at the one-hour observation point. These results captured acute functional disruption, not recovery.

06 / What I contributed

  • Optimized adult zebrafish retina preparation and CMOS-MEA tissue mounting.
  • Conducted extracellular electrophysiology recordings under spontaneous and light-stimulated conditions.
  • Implemented light-stimulation, pharmacological and light-lesion experimental workflows.
  • Processed spike data and analyzed firing rates, spike timing and stimulus-associated responses.
  • Compared experimental conditions and characterized initial RGC functional response classes.
  • Documented the workflow and findings in my MSc dissertation.

07 / Main takeaway and next steps

The project established a more reliable preparation for recording adult zebrafish retinal activity on high-density CMOS-MEAs and demonstrated that light-responsive neuronal activity could be measured and functionally characterized. It also showed how pharmacological treatment and acute retinal damage alter those signals.

The dissertation explicitly recommends repeating experiments to improve robustness and reproducibility. Studying functional recovery after retinal regeneration remains a future direction, not an outcome demonstrated by this project.
