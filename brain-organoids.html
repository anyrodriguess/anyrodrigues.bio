Brain Organoids

From iPSCs to assembloids and organ-on-chip systems

How human stem cells became three-dimensional models of neural development.

01 / Origins

Human induced pluripotent stem cells (iPSCs) made it possible to generate neural cells from reprogrammed adult cells. Takahashi and Yamanaka established reprogramming in mouse cells in 2006; human iPSCs followed in 2007. [1] [2]

02 / Cerebral organoids

Lancaster and colleagues developed cerebral organoids that self-organize aspects of early human brain development and modeled primary microcephaly. Organoids reproduce selected features of developing tissue; they are not miniature fully functional brains. [3]

03 / Assembloids

By combining region-specific neural spheroids, Birey and colleagues investigated interneuron migration and interactions between developing neural regions. [4]

04 / What limits these models?

Diffusion constraints, absent or incomplete vascularization, variable maturation and differences between batches remain major challenges. Microphysiological systems and perfused chips can help control culture conditions, but do not automatically solve every limitation. [5]

05 / Where electrophysiology enters

MEA recordings can complement cellular and molecular measurements by monitoring spontaneous activity, bursts, synchronization and stimulus responses. For closed-loop learning experiments, see Organoid Intelligence.
