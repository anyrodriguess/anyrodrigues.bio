Neural Plasticity

How neural responses change with experience

From short-term adaptation to stronger claims about learning.

01 / Plasticity is not one single phenomenon

Neural plasticity refers to changes in the properties of neurons, synapses or circuits. These changes occur across different timescales and may result from activity, development, injury or experience.

02 / What can be measured in vitro?

Repeated stimuli may produce changes in firing, bursts, synchronization or evoked responses. Such changes can suggest adaptation but do not, on their own, establish learning or memory.

03 / Closed-loop experiments

Closed-loop systems connect neural responses to subsequent input. They create experimental conditions in which researchers can test whether behavior changes in a task-dependent way. [1]

04 / Controls matter

Researchers must distinguish learning-like effects from fatigue, drift, spontaneous changes in excitability and electrode artifacts. Reproducible performance and well-designed controls are essential.

05 / Why it connects to OI

A central challenge for Organoid Intelligence is deciding which measurable changes constitute meaningful adaptive computation rather than generic network responses.
