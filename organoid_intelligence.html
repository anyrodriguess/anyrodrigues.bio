ORGANOID INTELLIGENCE

exploring living neural networks and computation

📁 what_is_organoid_intelligence.txt

Organoid Intelligence (OI) is an emerging field exploring whether three-dimensional cultures of human neural cells can be interfaced with technology and used to study — and potentially perform — forms of biological information processing and computation.

Brain organoids are laboratory-grown 3D neural tissues derived from stem cells. They do not reproduce a complete human brain, but they can develop organised neural populations and spontaneous electrical activity.

OI brings together stem-cell biology, neuroscience, electrophysiology, bioengineering, machine learning and brain-machine interfaces.

⚙ basic_loop.sys

One possibility I find particularly fascinating is creating a closed loop between living neural tissue and a computer. In such a system, the next stimulus depends on the neural activity recorded after the previous one.

COMPUTERprocesses responses / updates input
⇄
MEA INTERFACEstimulates / records
⇄
NEURAL TISSUEresponds through electrical activity

By recording a response and adjusting the next input, researchers can investigate adaptation and plasticity. This is a simplified conceptual diagram; actual systems vary in their stimulation and recording methods.

♥ why_i_am_here.txt

What interests me most about organoid intelligence is the possibility of moving beyond recording neuronal activity and beginning to interact with biological neural networks.

During my research at DZNE, I maintained olfactory neural co-cultures and recorded their electrical responses to odorants using microelectrode arrays. Later, at CRTD, I studied spontaneous and light-evoked activity in adult zebrafish retina using high-density MEAs.

These experiences made me curious about how neural networks process information, how their activity changes with stimulation, and whether those responses can help us build systems that interact with living neural tissue.

I am particularly interested in closed-loop electrophysiology, neural plasticity and translating biological network activity into meaningful computational output. Organoid intelligence is a direction I hope to explore, rather than a field I have worked in directly so far.
